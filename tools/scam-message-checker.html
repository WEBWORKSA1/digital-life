Scam message checker

Not sure about a text or email? Paste it below. The checker highlights the pressure tactics and technical tricks that scam messages rely on.

What it looks for

  • Urgency and threats, such as deadlines, locked accounts and final notices.
  • Requests for secrets: passwords, one-time codes, card or identity numbers.
  • Irreversible payments: gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers and payment apps.
  • Disguised links: shorteners, look-alike addresses and raw number addresses.
  • Too-good offers: prizes, refunds and inheritances you were not expecting.
  • Secrecy and remote access: being told not to tell anyone, or to install an app.

What it cannot do

The checker matches known patterns in the wording. It does not visit links, verify senders or detect a well-written, targeted scam. A clean result is not proof that a message is genuine. Treat it as a second opinion that trains your eye.

The rule that always works

Never act through the message itself. Close it, then reach the organisation using its official app, the number on your card or a web address you type yourself. If the problem is real, it will still be there.
