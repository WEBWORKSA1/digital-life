Passphrase generator

A handful of random words is easier to remember and type than a short jumble of symbols, and can be much harder to guess.

When to use a passphrase

Use one for the few secrets you must remember and type yourself: your password manager’s master password, your computer login and your phone or disk encryption password. Everything else should be a long random password generated and stored by the manager.

Why random words work

Security comes from the number of equally likely possibilities, not from how odd the result looks. Each word here is drawn at random from a fixed list using your browser’s cryptographic random number source, so every extra word multiplies the possibilities. Six or more words is a sound choice for a master password.

Do

  • Accept the words you are given. Choosing your own favourites removes the randomness.
  • Write a new master passphrase on paper and keep it somewhere safe until you know it by heart.
  • Turn on two-step login for the account it protects.

Do not

  • Use a quote, lyric or phrase from a book. Those are in cracking lists.
  • Reuse the same passphrase on more than one account.
