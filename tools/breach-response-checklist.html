Breach response checklist

A company you use has been breached, or a notification says your details were exposed. Work down this list. Your progress is saved in this browser.

  • Confirm it is real. Do not use links in the notification. Check the company’s own website or the breach radar.
  • Change the password on the breached account to a new, unique one.
  • Change it everywhere you reused it. Start with email, banking and shopping accounts.
  • Turn on two-step login for the breached account and for your email.
  • Sign out of all sessions and remove devices or apps you do not recognise.
  • Check account recovery details. Make sure the backup email and phone number are still yours.
  • If card or bank details were exposed, call your bank, ask for a replacement card and turn on transaction alerts.
  • If identity numbers were exposed, place a fraud alert or credit freeze with the credit bureaus in your country.
  • Review recent statements for charges you do not recognise, including small test amounts.
  • Expect targeted phishing. Scammers use breach details to sound convincing for months afterwards.
  • Accept free monitoring if the company offers it, by signing up through its official site.
  • Record what happened: date, what was exposed and what you changed.
