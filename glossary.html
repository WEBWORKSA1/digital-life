Reference

Glossary

Forty terms that appear in settings screens, news stories and scam warnings, explained without jargon.

2FA (two-factor authentication)

A second proof, such as a code or key, required after your password. Stops most takeovers even when a password leaks.

Ad tracker

Code embedded in sites and apps that records what you view so advertisers can build a profile of you.

Authenticator app

An app that generates short-lived login codes on your phone. Safer than codes sent by text message.

Backup

A separate copy of your files that survives loss, theft or failure of the original device.

Backup codes

One-time codes issued when you enable two-step login, used if you lose your phone. Keep them offline.

Biometrics

Unlocking with a fingerprint or face. Convenient, and the data normally stays on your device.

Botnet

A network of hijacked devices controlled remotely, often built from unpatched routers and cameras.

Cookie

A small file a website stores in your browser to remember you. Third-party cookies follow you between sites.

Credential stuffing

Trying usernames and passwords leaked from one site on many others. It only works when passwords are reused.

Dark pattern

A design trick that pushes you into something you did not intend, such as a hidden cancel button.

Data breach

An incident in which information held by an organisation is accessed or published without permission.

Data broker

A company that collects personal details from many sources and sells them. Many offer an opt-out on request.

Deepfake

Audio, images or video generated by AI to imitate a real person, increasingly used in impersonation scams.

Digital footprint

The trail of data created by your activity online, both what you post and what is collected about you.

Digital legacy

What happens to your accounts, files and online presence after death or incapacity.

Doomscrolling

Continuing to scroll through distressing content long after it has stopped being useful.

Encryption

Scrambling data so only someone with the key can read it.

End-to-end encryption

Encryption in which only the sender and recipient hold the keys, so the service provider cannot read the content.

Firmware

The built-in software that runs devices such as routers, cameras and TVs. It needs updates too.

Identity theft

Use of your personal details to open accounts, borrow money or claim benefits in your name.

Large language model (LLM)

The type of AI behind chat assistants. It predicts plausible text, which is why it can sound confident and still be wrong.

Legacy contact

A person you nominate inside a service to manage or download your account after you die.

Malware

Software built to harm: stealing data, spying, or locking files for ransom.

Metadata

Data about data, such as who you messaged and when, or where a photo was taken.

Passkey

A login that replaces the password with a key stored on your device and unlocked by fingerprint, face or PIN. It cannot be phished in the usual way.

Passphrase

A password made of several random words. Long, strong and easier to type than symbol soup.

Password manager

An app that creates, stores and fills a unique password for every account behind one master passphrase.

Phishing

A message that impersonates a trusted sender to steal logins, codes or money.

Ransomware

Malware that encrypts your files and demands payment. Offline backups are the defence.

Security key

A small hardware device used as a second factor. The strongest widely available protection for an account.

SIM swap

A fraud in which your phone number is moved to an attacker’s SIM so they receive your text codes.

Smishing

Phishing by text message, commonly fake delivery, toll or bank alerts.

Social engineering

Manipulating people, not machines, into handing over access or money.

Stalkerware

Hidden apps that let someone else monitor your location, messages or calls.

Subscription creep

The slow build-up of recurring charges you no longer notice or use.

3-2-1 backup

Three copies of your data, on two kinds of storage, with one copy kept somewhere else.

VPN

A service that routes your traffic through an encrypted tunnel. Useful on public Wi-Fi, not a cure-all for privacy.

Vishing

Phishing by phone call, often with spoofed caller ID or a cloned voice.

WPA3

The current Wi-Fi security standard. Use it, or WPA2, and never leave a network open.

Zero-day

A software flaw exploited before a fix exists. The reason prompt updates matter.
