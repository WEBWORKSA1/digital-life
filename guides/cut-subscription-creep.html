Cut subscription creep for good

Subscriptions are designed to be forgotten. Each one is small, each renews silently, and together they often form one of the larger lines in a household budget.

Reviewed November 2026 · 5 minute read

Step 1: Build the full list

People consistently underestimate what they pay because charges are spread across cards, app stores and payment services. Check all four places:

  1. Email. Search for receipt, renewal, subscription, membership and trial.
  2. App stores. Open the subscriptions section of your phone account.
  3. Statements. Scan three months of every card and bank account for repeated amounts. Include annual charges by looking back twelve months.
  4. Payment services and any card details saved in browsers.

Enter each one in the subscription audit to see the true monthly and ten-year totals.

Step 2: Keep, pause or cancel

Ask two questions of every line: have I used it in the past 30 days, and would I sign up again today at this price? Two yeses, keep. One, downgrade or pause. None, cancel.

Watch for overlap: several video services at once, two cloud storage plans, a music subscription bundled in a plan you already pay for, or family members paying separately for something with a shared tier.

Step 3: Cancel cleanly

  • Cancel where you subscribed. A subscription bought through an app store must be cancelled there, not by deleting the app.
  • Expect friction. Extra screens, discount offers and hard-to-find buttons are deliberate. Keep going until you see a confirmation.
  • Save the confirmation email or a screenshot with the date.
  • Check the next statement. If a charge appears after cancellation, dispute it with the confirmation as evidence.

Step 4: Rotate instead of stacking

For entertainment, subscribe to one service for a month or two, watch what you came for, cancel and move to the next. You keep access to everything over a year for a fraction of the cost.

Free trial rule: the moment you start a trial, set a calendar reminder for two days before it ends. Many services let you cancel immediately and still use the remaining trial period.

Step 5: Make it hard to creep back

  • Put all subscriptions on one card so they appear in one place.
  • Turn on transaction alerts for that card.
  • Prefer monthly billing while you are unsure, and annual only for services you have used for a year.
  • Repeat the audit every six months; put it in your calendar now.

Do not forget the security side

Old accounts with saved cards are a risk as well as a cost. When you cancel something for good, remove the stored payment method and delete the account if you can.
