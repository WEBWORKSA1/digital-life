Secure your home Wi-Fi and devices in 20 minutes

Your router is the front door for every phone, laptop, TV and camera in the house. Most are still running the settings they left the factory with.

Reviewed November 2026 · 5 minute read

1. Change the router’s admin password

This is different from the Wi-Fi password. It protects the settings page, and default admin logins are published online. Open the router’s app or settings page (the address is usually on a sticker on the device), find administration or system settings, and set a long unique password stored in your password manager.

2. Update the firmware and turn on auto-update

Firmware is the router’s operating system. Flaws are found regularly and unpatched routers are recruited into botnets. Install the latest version and enable automatic updates if offered. If the manufacturer no longer issues updates for your model, plan to replace it.

3. Use WPA3 or WPA2 with a strong Wi-Fi password

In wireless security settings choose WPA3, or the WPA2/WPA3 mixed mode if older devices cannot connect. Avoid WEP and open networks entirely. Use a passphrase of four or more random words; it is easy to share with guests and hard to guess.

4. Put visitors and smart gadgets on a guest network

A guest network gives internet access without access to your computers and files. Use it for visitors and for smart TVs, speakers, plugs and cameras. If one of those devices is compromised, it cannot reach your laptop.

5. Switch off what you do not use

  • WPS, the push-button pairing feature, has known weaknesses.
  • Remote administration from the internet, unless you truly need it.
  • UPnP, if no device in your home depends on it.

6. Update everything that connects

Turn on automatic updates for phones, computers, browsers, TVs and smart devices. Updates are the single most effective defence against the flaws criminals exploit, and most attacks use weaknesses for which a fix already exists.

Two things that do not help much: hiding your network name and filtering by device address. Both are easily bypassed and mostly create inconvenience for you.

On public Wi-Fi

Modern sites and apps encrypt their traffic, so public Wi-Fi is less dangerous than it once was. Still, prefer your phone’s hotspot for banking, decline prompts to install certificates or apps, and make sure your device does not auto-join open networks.

Before you sell or recycle a device

Sign out of your accounts, remove it from your account’s device list and run a full factory reset. For computers, confirm disk encryption was on before wiping.
