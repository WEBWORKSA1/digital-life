The 30-minute privacy checkup

Privacy is not about hiding. It is about deciding who knows what. Half an hour in the right settings screens changes more than any app you could install.

Reviewed November 2026 · 6 minute read

  • Minutes 0 to 8: app permissions. In your phone’s privacy settings, review location, microphone, camera, contacts and photos. Set location to “while using” or “never” and remove anything an app has no clear need for.
  • Minutes 8 to 11: delete unused apps. Every installed app is a data collector and an update you must maintain.
  • Minutes 11 to 15: location history. In your phone and mapping account settings, pause location history or set it to delete automatically.
  • Minutes 15 to 19: ad personalisation. Turn off personalised ads in your phone settings and in the ad settings of your main accounts. Reset the advertising identifier.
  • Minutes 19 to 23: browser. Block third-party cookies, turn on tracking protection and review which sites may send notifications or use your location.
  • Minutes 23 to 27: social profiles. Set who can see your posts, friend list, phone number and email. Review apps connected to your account and remove old ones.
  • Minutes 27 to 30: search for yourself. Search your name, phone number and address. Note any people-search or data broker sites that list you and use their opt-out forms.

Why permissions come first

Permissions are the taps through which data flows. An app with constant location access knows where you live, work, worship and sleep. Most apps work perfectly well with access only while open, or with none.

What ad settings do and do not do

Switching off personalisation does not reduce the number of ads. It limits the use of your activity to target them and reduces how widely that profile is shared. Combined with blocking third-party cookies, it meaningfully shrinks your trail.

Data brokers

Data brokers compile public records and commercial data into profiles that are sold on. Many must honour opt-out requests, and some regions give you a legal right to deletion. Opt-outs are tedious and listings can reappear, so set a reminder to repeat the search every six months.

High-value extras if you have ten more minutes: use a private email alias for sign-ups, switch messaging with close contacts to an end-to-end encrypted app, and remove location data from photos before posting them publicly.

Keep it realistic

Perfect privacy is not available to anyone who uses a phone. The aim is proportion: share deliberately with services that earn it, and close the taps that run by default.
